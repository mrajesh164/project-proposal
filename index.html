Which recipe should I actually bake?

I want to make an app for home bakers that consolidates and recommends recipes, to make choosing one and following it easier.

The problem

When I bake something I haven't made in a while, I don't open one recipe. I open five. Two pastry books for cream puffs, one in metric and one in cups. A handful of chocolate chip cookie recipes where the butter and flour amounts all differ a little.

The differences aren't mistakes. More butter makes a cookie spread flatter and chewier. More flour makes it cakier. Someone picked those numbers on purpose. But that only helps if you can see the numbers next to each other, and you can't. They're in five tabs, in three different unit systems, at the bottom of a long blog post.

Searching up that many recipes and then deciding which measurements to use is overstimulating and confusing. That's the actual problem. Finding a recipe was never the hard part.

What it does

You search for what you're making and how you want it to turn out, like chocolate chip cookies, chewy. It finds the recipes for that dish, puts every measurement on the same scale, and shows each ingredient as a range across all of them, with what the spread does.

Cups or grams, your choice. Not everyone owns a scale, and not every recipe is written for one, so you can switch the whole view between weight and volume. The comparison still runs in grams underneath. A cup of flour can be anywhere from 120 to 145 g depending on how hard you packed it, so comparing recipes in cups would show agreement that isn't really there.

It recommends a real recipe, not a blend. A published recipe has been tested by the person who wrote it and by everyone in the comments. An average of five recipes hasn't been tested by anyone. So the app points at one that already exists, and shows the ranges behind the choice in case you want to pick a different one.

You can save what you bake. Once you've picked a recipe, it goes in your account, so the cookies you made in November are still there in February and you don't have to search for them again. The saved collection is the part I'm most interested in building out. Once the app knows what you actually bake and how you like it, the recommendations stop being generic and start being yours.

Who it's for

Home bakers who find their recipes online instead of in books, and who care how something turns out, not just that it works. The people I'm building this around bake somewhere between once a week and once in a while. Often enough to have opinions about texture, not often enough to have the ratios memorized.

My focus: Design

The problem here is cognitive load, not computation. Once you find the structured data, parsing a recipe is straightforward. The hard part is putting five recipes on one screen so someone can take them in quickly and decide. That's an interface problem.

Going deep on design means the comparison view itself: showing the differences that matter and letting the rest recede. Normalizing units so the numbers are actually comparable. The states for when a dish only has two recipes, or none, or when a recipe is missing half its structured data. And making the comparison grid work with a screen reader.

It also has to be fun. Baking is something people do because they enjoy it, and a tool that feels like a spreadsheet is a tool people close. I want this to feel good to use, not just correct.

And it has to work where baking actually happens. People will open this standing in a kitchen with flour on their hands and the phone propped against something, reading it from a foot away while the butter softens. That rules out small tap targets, long scrolls, and anything that needs two hands or careful aim.

Roadmap

Nov 17 v1 shipsWorks end to end: search a dish, pick a texture, see the ranges, get a recipe.

Nov 24–Dec 1 Build v2Widen past cookies to other baked goods. A texture control you can slide instead of a fixed choice. Saved comparisons.
Dec 1 v2, at the fair

In v1

  • Chocolate chip cookies, then other cookies
  • Search by dish and texture
  • A seeded recipe corpus
  • Ingredient ranges, in grams or cups
  • One recommended real recipe
  • Accounts, and saving the recipes you pick

Waits for v2

  • Other baked goods
  • A movable texture control
  • Recommendations based on what you've saved
  • Saved comparisons, not just saved recipes
  • Live search beyond the corpus

How I'll know it works

Two things I can measure. First, how many of the recipes I crawl give me complete, usable structured data. Second, the actual job: give someone a dish and a texture, and time how long it takes them to pick a recipe and explain why, compared to doing it with five tabs.
